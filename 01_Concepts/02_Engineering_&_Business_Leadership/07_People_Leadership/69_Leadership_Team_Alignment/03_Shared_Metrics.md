# Shared Metrics

## Purpose
Develop a practical, senior-leadership understanding of **Shared Metrics** and use it to improve people, teams, organizations, and business outcomes.

## Core Model
- **Outcome:** what business, team, customer, or people outcome is being improved.
- **People:** who is affected, what motivates them, what capability exists, and what development is needed.
- **Context:** organizational stage, urgency, culture, incentives, role maturity, and constraints.
- **Leader behavior:** what the leader says, decides, models, reinforces, delegates, or changes.
- **System effect:** how incentives, structures, processes, and decisions shape behavior over time.

## Leadership Questions
1. What problem are we actually trying to solve?
2. Is this a person problem, role problem, team problem, system problem, or strategy problem?
3. What behavior is expected and how is it reinforced?
4. What decision rights and accountability should exist?
5. What evidence would show the intervention worked?
6. What unintended consequence could the leadership action create?

## People and Performance
Make expectations explicit. Separate:
- capability from motivation;
- performance from potential;
- intent from impact;
- behavior from personality;
- coaching from performance management;
- accountability from blame.

Use observable evidence and documented expectations for important people decisions.

## Communication
Adapt message, detail, tone, and channel to the audience. In high-stakes situations:
- state facts and known uncertainty;
- explain impact;
- make the decision or ask clear;
- identify owner and next step;
- close the feedback loop.

Listen for both explicit statements and signals such as silence, repeated disagreement, avoidance, attrition patterns, or decision delays.

## Decision and Delegation
Match authority with accountability. Define what is:
- fully delegated;
- delegated with guardrails;
- consulted;
- escalated;
- reserved for executive decision.

Prefer reversible local decisions and fast feedback where risk allows. Do not create approval bottlenecks merely to preserve managerial control.

## Culture and Trust
Leaders create culture through repeated behavior and incentives. Trust grows through competence, reliability, integrity, transparency, and consistent follow-through. Repair broken trust by acknowledging reality, correcting behavior, and demonstrating change over time.

## Conflict and Difficult Situations
Distinguish healthy disagreement from destructive conflict. Address behavior and impact directly, preserve dignity, investigate facts, and document important decisions. Escalate when safety, legal, ethical, retaliation, or serious organizational risk is involved.

## Metrics and Evidence
Useful evidence may include performance outcomes, engagement signals, attrition, hiring funnel data, time-to-productivity, promotion patterns, delivery health, decision latency, manager effectiveness, and qualitative feedback. Metrics are signals rather than complete definitions of leadership quality.

## Organizational Design
Consider whether structure, reporting lines, decision rights, incentives, and team boundaries help or hinder the desired outcome. Many apparent people problems are actually caused by ambiguous ownership, overloaded managers, conflicting goals, poor interfaces, or insufficient organizational capacity.

## Executive Considerations
At senior levels, optimize for enterprise outcomes rather than functional optimization. Consider:
- strategy;
- talent;
- capital and capacity;
- organizational leverage;
- risk;
- customer impact;
- culture;
- execution speed;
- long-term capability.

## Common Mistakes
- Solving organizational problems with individual coaching alone.
- Avoiding difficult feedback until review cycles.
- Confusing kindness with low standards.
- Confusing high standards with fear-based management.
- Delegating tasks without decision authority.
- Using metrics as targets without understanding gaming behavior.
- Reorganizing without fixing the underlying problem.
- Communicating late because the leader wants perfect information.

## Ethical and Legal Awareness
People decisions can create serious ethical, legal, and organizational consequences. Partner with HR/legal specialists for jurisdiction-specific matters and protect confidentiality. Avoid retaliation, discrimination, conflicts of interest, and informal processes that bypass established protections.

## Staff / Executive View
Leadership is a leverage discipline. The objective is not to personally solve every problem, but to create people, decision systems, incentives, structures, and culture that repeatedly produce strong outcomes without depending on one heroic leader.

# Meeting Purpose

## Purpose
Develop a durable leadership and culture mental model for **Meeting Purpose**, with emphasis on how leadership behavior, incentives, systems, and decisions shape organizational outcomes.

## Core Model
- **Principle:** what leadership or cultural principle is being applied?
- **Behavior:** what observable actions demonstrate it?
- **System:** what hiring, incentives, processes, decision rights, or rituals reinforce it?
- **Signal:** what do employees/customers/partners actually experience?
- **Outcome:** what business, people, execution, or trust outcome should result?

## Key Questions
1. What behavior does this principle encourage?
2. What behavior does it accidentally discourage?
3. Which incentives reinforce or undermine it?
4. What happens when the principle conflicts with another important principle?
5. How does it behave under pressure, rapid growth, failure, and crisis?
6. What evidence would show that the culture is real rather than merely stated?

## Leadership Application
A strong leader translates principles into:
- explicit expectations;
- decision rights;
- operating mechanisms;
- hiring and promotion criteria;
- feedback and recognition;
- visible role modeling;
- consistent consequences.

## Failure Modes
Watch for:
- values posted but not lived;
- incentives rewarding contradictory behavior;
- leaders exempting themselves from standards;
- fear suppressing bad news;
- accountability becoming blame;
- psychological safety becoming avoidance of standards;
- transparency becoming indiscriminate information dumping;
- high performance becoming unsustainable;
- culture becoming personality-dependent.

## Under Pressure
Examine how the principle behaves during:
- missed targets;
- major incidents;
- layoffs or restructuring;
- customer escalation;
- product failure;
- ethical dilemma;
- rapid hiring;
- executive disagreement;
- M&A or transformation.

## Measurement and Evidence
Useful evidence may include employee feedback, retention/attrition patterns, promotion patterns, hiring outcomes, decision latency, execution quality, customer outcomes, incident learning, internal mobility, and qualitative observations. Metrics should be treated as signals rather than perfect representations of culture.

## Communication
Leadership culture becomes credible when executives explain:
- what the organization values;
- what tradeoffs are expected;
- what behavior is unacceptable;
- how difficult decisions are made;
- how mistakes are handled;
- what employees can expect from leaders.

## Embedded / Engineering Leadership Context
For a technology organization, connect culture to engineering review quality, technical ownership, safety/security escalation, incident learning, architecture governance, customer impact, quality discipline, and long-term technical investment.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating culture as an HR-only topic.
- Confusing friendliness with trust.
- Confusing psychological safety with low accountability.
- Rewarding heroics while claiming to value sustainable systems.
- Promoting strong individual contributors who damage team health.
- Measuring engagement while ignoring incentive structures.
- Declaring values without changing leadership behavior.

## CEO / Executive Perspective
At scale, culture is an organizational control system. Leadership decisions about people, priorities, incentives, information flow, accountability, customer focus, and capital allocation continuously teach the organization what actually matters.

## Personal Leadership Journal
Record:
- the principle;
- a real situation;
- your decision;
- what signal you sent;
- the unintended consequence;
- what you would repeat;
- what you would change.

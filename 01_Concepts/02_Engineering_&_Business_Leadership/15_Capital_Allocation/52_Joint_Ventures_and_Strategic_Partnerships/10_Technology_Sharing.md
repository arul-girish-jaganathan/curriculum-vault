# Technology Sharing

## Purpose
Develop a practical executive understanding of **Technology Sharing** so capital decisions improve long-term enterprise value rather than optimizing a single financial metric.

## Core Model
- **Capital:** what resources are being committed and for how long.
- **Return:** what incremental cash flows, strategic value, or risk reduction are expected.
- **Opportunity cost:** what the same capital could produce elsewhere.
- **Risk:** what assumptions could fail and how downside is contained.
- **Decision rights:** who approves, owns, monitors, and can stop the investment.

## Key Questions
1. What business objective requires capital?
2. What are the incremental cash flows and non-financial strategic effects?
3. What is the relevant cost of capital or hurdle rate?
4. What is the opportunity cost versus the next-best alternative?
5. What evidence would justify increasing, maintaining, staging, or stopping the investment?

## Investment Case
A strong case separates:
- baseline vs incremental economics;
- assumptions vs observed facts;
- one-time vs recurring effects;
- accounting profit vs cash flow;
- nominal growth vs value-creating growth;
- strategic option value vs guaranteed return.

## Risk and Scenarios
Evaluate base, upside, downside, and stress cases. Identify break-even thresholds, major sensitivities, liquidity exposure, execution risks, and assumptions that could invalidate the return thesis.

## Capital Alternatives
Compare relevant alternatives such as:
- organic reinvestment;
- debt reduction;
- acquisitions;
- partnerships;
- buybacks;
- dividends;
- cash retention;
- staged or pilot investment.

Do not compare alternatives only on headline return. Include timing, risk, strategic control, reversibility, liquidity, and long-term optionality.

## Measurement
Track expected vs actual:
- revenue and margin;
- free cash flow;
- invested capital;
- ROIC/ROE where relevant;
- payback;
- project milestones;
- working capital;
- capital productivity;
- strategic outcomes;
- risk reduction.

## Governance
Define investment thresholds, approval rights, stage gates, kill criteria, post-investment reviews, exception handling, and accountability. A capital allocation system should make it easier to stop weak investments early.

## Common Biases
Watch for:
- sunk-cost thinking;
- escalation of commitment;
- overconfidence;
- confirmation bias;
- anchoring;
- empire building;
- short-termism;
- narrative-driven assumptions;
- false precision.

## Technology / Engineering Context
For technology-intensive companies, consider R&D, platform investment, talent, cloud/infrastructure, cybersecurity, manufacturing capacity, product investment, technical debt, acquisitions, and build/buy/partner choices as competing uses of scarce capital.

## Executive View
Capital allocation is not merely finance. It is the mechanism by which strategy becomes resource allocation. A CEO should be able to connect market opportunity, product strategy, engineering capability, organizational capacity, financial returns, risk, and long-term compounding.

## Staff/CEO-Level Reasoning
The strongest decision is not necessarily the project with the highest modeled IRR. It is the decision that creates the best risk-adjusted, strategically durable value while preserving the company's ability to invest in future opportunities.

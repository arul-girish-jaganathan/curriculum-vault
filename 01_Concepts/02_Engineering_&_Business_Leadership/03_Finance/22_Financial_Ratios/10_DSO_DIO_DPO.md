# DSO DIO DPO

## Purpose
Build a practical executive-level understanding of **DSO DIO DPO** and connect the financial concept to business performance, cash generation, capital allocation, risk, and strategic decisions.

## Core Model
- **Economic driver:** identify the underlying business activity that creates or consumes value.
- **Financial statement effect:** identify where the concept appears in the income statement, balance sheet, or cash-flow statement.
- **Cash effect:** distinguish accounting profit from actual cash movement.
- **Capital effect:** identify how the decision changes invested capital, financing needs, or shareholder value.
- **Decision use:** state which management, investment, pricing, operating, or strategic decision the metric should inform.

## Key Questions
1. What business driver moves this number?
2. Is the effect on profit, cash, capital, risk, or some combination?
3. What assumptions make the analysis reliable?
4. What would cause the metric to improve while the underlying business gets worse?
5. What decision should an executive make from the analysis?

## Analysis
Use the appropriate evidence:
- financial statements and footnotes;
- management reporting;
- budgets and forecasts;
- unit economics;
- cash-flow analysis;
- capital investment cases;
- debt and covenant information;
- market and valuation data.

Separate reported numbers, management adjustments, normalized economics, and forward-looking assumptions.

## Common Pitfalls
- Confusing revenue growth with value creation.
- Treating EBITDA as cash flow.
- Ignoring working-capital investment.
- Using a ratio without understanding the underlying business model.
- Comparing companies with incompatible accounting policies or capital intensity.
- Using a valuation model with false precision.
- Optimizing a metric while damaging the economic system behind it.

## Executive View
A useful finance analysis answers:
- **Where does the money come from?**
- **Where does it go?**
- **How much capital is required?**
- **What return does that capital earn?**
- **What risks could change the outcome?**
- **What should management do next?**

## Engineering / Technology Connection
For technology companies, connect finance to R&D productivity, engineering headcount, product margins, compute/cloud costs, platform reuse, technical debt, manufacturing economics, warranty/service costs, product lifecycle, and capital allocation.

## Decision Framework
1. Define the business objective.
2. Establish the baseline and relevant financial metric.
3. Identify the operating drivers.
4. Model scenarios and sensitivities.
5. Quantify cash, capital, and risk effects.
6. Compare against alternatives and required returns.
7. Decide, monitor actuals, and update assumptions.

## Governance and Evidence
For material decisions, preserve assumptions, model versions, approvals, source data, scenario ranges, and post-investment results. Financial discipline requires traceability between the decision and the realized outcome.

## Staff / CEO Perspective
Finance is not just reporting history. It is the language for deciding where scarce capital, people, time, and risk should be deployed to create durable economic value.

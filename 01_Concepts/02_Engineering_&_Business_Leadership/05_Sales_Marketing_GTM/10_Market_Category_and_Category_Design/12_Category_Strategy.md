# Category Strategy

## Purpose
Build a practical, executive-grade understanding of **Category Strategy** as part of a repeatable sales, marketing, and go-to-market system.

## Core Model
- **Customer:** who has the problem, who uses the product, who pays, and who influences the decision.
- **Value:** what measurable customer outcome justifies action and spend.
- **Motion:** how demand is created, captured, converted, expanded, and retained.
- **Economics:** revenue, margin, acquisition cost, sales cost, payback, retention, and lifetime value where relevant.
- **Evidence:** what customer, funnel, pipeline, win/loss, or financial data supports the decision.

## Strategic Questions
1. Which customer segment and use case are being targeted?
2. Why will that customer choose this offering over alternatives?
3. How does the buyer discover, evaluate, approve, purchase, adopt, and renew?
4. Which GTM motion is economically and operationally appropriate?
5. What leading indicators predict revenue, retention, or expansion?

## Customer and Buyer
Map the ICP, segments, personas, buying center, problem/trigger, decision criteria, economic impact, objections, alternatives, and approval/procurement/legal path.

## Funnel and Revenue Mechanics
Consider awareness, demand generation, lead capture, qualification, discovery, evaluation, proposal, negotiation, close, onboarding, adoption, renewal, and expansion. Measure conversion and velocity between stages.

## Economics and Metrics
Use metrics appropriate to the motion, including pipeline, conversion, win rate, sales cycle, ACV/ASP/ARR where applicable, CAC, payback, gross margin, retention, churn, NRR/expansion, forecast accuracy, and marketing efficiency.

## Failure Modes
Watch for weak ICP, poor positioning, low-quality demand, excessive discounting, stalled evaluations, weak forecasting, channel conflict, onboarding failure, poor adoption, unmanaged churn, and GTM complexity that exceeds market economics.

## Experimentation
Test meaningful hypotheses around segmentation, messaging, pricing, packaging, outreach, campaigns, channels, onboarding, and expansion with measurable acceptance criteria.

## Enterprise and Complex Deals
For complex B2B sales, map stakeholders, economic value, decision process, decision criteria, procurement/legal/security gates, competition, champion strength, executive sponsorship, mutual actions, and implementation risk.

## Channel and International Considerations
Evaluate reach, control, margin, enablement, localization, regulatory needs, partner incentives, customer ownership, channel conflict, working capital, and country economics.

## Technology and Data
Treat CRM, marketing automation, analytics, enrichment, sales engagement, CPQ, intent data, and AI as parts of the GTM operating system. Data quality, privacy, governance, and workflow consistency matter.

## Common Mistakes
- Confusing product-market fit with sales execution.
- Targeting everyone instead of an economically attractive ICP.
- Measuring activity or leads instead of qualified pipeline and profitable revenue.
- Discounting to hide weak value communication.
- Forecasting from hope rather than stage evidence.
- Optimizing acquisition while ignoring retention and expansion.

## CEO-Level View
Treat GTM as a business system connecting market selection, product value, positioning, demand, sales motion, pricing, distribution, onboarding, retention, expansion, organization, technology, and capital allocation. The objective is durable, profitable, repeatable growth.

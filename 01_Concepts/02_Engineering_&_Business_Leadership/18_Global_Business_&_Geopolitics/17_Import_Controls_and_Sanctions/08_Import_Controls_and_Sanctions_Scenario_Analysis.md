# Import Controls and Sanctions Scenario Analysis

## Purpose
Build a durable understanding of **Import Controls and Sanctions Scenario Analysis** as it affects companies operating across countries, markets, technologies, supply chains, capital markets, and regulatory environments.

## Core Model
- **Actors:** companies, governments, regulators, alliances, suppliers, customers, investors, labor, and international institutions.
- **Interests:** economic growth, security, market access, technological advantage, employment, fiscal stability, and strategic autonomy.
- **Mechanisms:** trade policy, regulation, subsidies, sanctions, investment screening, standards, capital controls, diplomacy, and market forces.
- **Business effect:** revenue access, cost, margin, capital availability, supply continuity, technology access, reputation, and optionality.

## Key Questions
1. Who has the power to change the operating environment?
2. What economic or strategic interest is driving that actor?
3. Which business dependency creates exposure?
4. What are the first-order and second-order effects?
5. Which decisions are reversible, and which create long-lived lock-in?
6. What indicators would show that the scenario is changing?

## Risk and Scenario Analysis
Separate:
- structural risk from event risk;
- political intent from policy capability;
- announced policy from implemented policy;
- direct effects from second-order effects;
- temporary disruption from structural change.

Use base, upside, downside, and stress scenarios. Identify triggers, leading indicators, decision points, and contingency actions.

## Business Translation
Connect the external environment to:
- market access;
- revenue and pricing;
- gross margin and operating margin;
- supply continuity;
- manufacturing footprint;
- inventory and working capital;
- capital expenditure;
- technology access;
- workforce and talent;
- compliance cost;
- valuation and strategic optionality.

## Capital and Financial Effects
Consider exchange rates, interest rates, inflation, commodity prices, capital flows, sovereign risk, funding costs, and investment screening when relevant.

## Technology and Supply Chain Effects
For technology companies, consider semiconductor dependencies, cloud/data-center geography, telecommunications, critical minerals, export controls, intellectual property, standards, talent flows, and supplier concentration.

## Governance and Compliance
Distinguish:
- law/regulation from policy announcements;
- mandatory obligations from voluntary commitments;
- domestic requirements from extraterritorial effects;
- compliance risk from strategic risk.

Maintain clear ownership, escalation paths, decision records, and auditable evidence.

## Common Blind Spots
- Assuming globalization always moves in one direction.
- Treating geopolitical headlines as equivalent to business impact.
- Ignoring second-order effects.
- Focusing on probability while ignoring impact magnitude.
- Underestimating country or supplier concentration.
- Treating announced policy as operationally complete.
- Ignoring incentives of the other side of a negotiation.

## CEO-Level View
Translate geopolitics into decisions about where to invest, source, manufacture, hire, sell, partner, acquire, exit, and hold strategic optionality. Avoid ideological thinking; focus on incentives, constraints, probabilities, consequences, and decision quality.

# Cost of Delay

## Purpose
Build an executive-grade understanding of **Cost of Delay** as a repeatable decision capability.

## Core Model
- **Objective:** define the outcome the decision is intended to improve.
- **Alternatives:** identify credible options, including the option to wait or do nothing.
- **Criteria:** define value, risk, cost, time, strategic fit, reversibility, and stakeholder consequences.
- **Uncertainty:** separate known facts, estimates, assumptions, and unknowns.
- **Decision rights:** identify who recommends, decides, executes, and reviews.
- **Evidence:** identify what information materially changes the decision.

## Decision Questions
1. What problem are we actually solving?
2. What would have to be true for each option to succeed?
3. What are the important assumptions and base rates?
4. What are the upside, downside, second-order effects, and opportunity costs?
5. Is the decision reversible, and what is the cost of waiting?
6. What evidence would change the recommendation?

## Process
1. Frame the problem.
2. Define objectives and constraints.
3. Generate realistic alternatives.
4. Identify key assumptions and uncertainties.
5. Estimate outcomes and risks.
6. Identify the most decision-relevant information.
7. Choose the appropriate decision mechanism.
8. Decide at the right level and speed.
9. Communicate the decision and rationale.
10. Monitor leading signals and revisit conditions when predefined triggers occur.

## Tradeoffs
Evaluate:
- short-term vs long-term value;
- growth vs profitability;
- speed vs quality;
- risk vs reward;
- local vs global optimization;
- centralization vs autonomy;
- build vs buy vs partner;
- innovation vs execution;
- control vs organizational velocity.

## Cognitive Discipline
Watch for:
- anchoring;
- confirmation bias;
- base-rate neglect;
- sunk-cost thinking;
- overconfidence;
- groupthink;
- availability and recency bias;
- outcome bias;
- planning fallacy;
- action bias and inaction bias.

Use disconfirming evidence and explicitly record assumptions rather than allowing them to remain implicit.

## Decision Quality
A good process can still produce a bad outcome because reality contains randomness. Evaluate:
- quality of the information available at decision time;
- quality of the reasoning;
- calibration of uncertainty;
- appropriateness of the decision process;
- execution against the intended decision.

Do not judge process quality only from the final outcome.

## Communication
A strong decision record states:
- context;
- problem;
- decision owner;
- alternatives;
- decision criteria;
- recommendation;
- important tradeoffs;
- assumptions;
- risks;
- expected signals;
- next actions;
- conditions for revisiting the decision.

## Review and Learning
After execution:
- compare expected vs actual outcomes;
- separate luck from controllable causes;
- identify which assumptions were wrong;
- update decision rules or operating mechanisms;
- record reusable lessons.

## Leadership Context
Senior decisions frequently cross technology, product, finance, people, operations, customers, safety, security, compliance, and reputation. Avoid optimizing one function while damaging the system-level objective.

## Common Mistakes
- Solving the wrong problem.
- Collecting information without a stop rule.
- Treating opinions as equally valuable despite different evidence.
- Delaying reversible decisions unnecessarily.
- Making irreversible commitments without explicit risk analysis.
- Confusing consensus with correctness.
- Overfitting to a single forecast.
- Failing to define who owns the decision.

## Staff / Executive View
Decision making is an engineering capability. Build explicit decision rights, evidence standards, escalation thresholds, feedback loops, post-decision learning, and governance so the organization can make high-quality decisions repeatedly at scale.

## CEO View
At the highest level, judgment is allocating scarce capital, people, attention, time, and risk across competing opportunities while preserving the company's ability to adapt. The objective is not certainty; it is consistently making better bets under uncertainty.
